# Renew SSE with overlapping connections

On 2026-10-03 the owner chose to retain SSE and establish a replacement stream before closing the current stream during normal renewal, and authorized publishing the agreed plan as the replacement specification in [issue #23](https://github.com/SimianW/share-tally/issues/23). The aim is to cover replacement authentication and connection setup with the still-valid current stream, reducing renewal gaps beyond what immediate reconnection after closure can achieve. The owner accepts the additional connection coordination in exchange for that continuity.

The agreed handoff pattern is to keep the current stream until the replacement receives `ready`, then close the current stream. Notifications continue to invalidate authenticated reads rather than apply financial changes, as required by [issue #19](https://github.com/SimianW/share-tally/issues/19).

The owner confirmed the following scope and lifecycle decisions during the design interview:

- All existing group and member event streams use the same handoff mechanism. Within one authenticated tab, consumers of the same group share its notification stream while retaining their own view-specific reads. A notification channel has at most one current connection and one replacement connection; mounted components do not each create their own connection pair.
- The server retains the current maximum stream lifetime of 30 seconds, bounded further by verified JWT expiry. It announces renewal five seconds before the actual deadline. This lead time is an initial choice to validate, not a guarantee that every network can complete handoff before expiry.
- A hidden tab closes its connections and suspends connection attempts, renewal and retries. Returning to the foreground establishes subscriptions before obtaining current view snapshots and preserves unsent form input. Background event delivery is not promised.
- Each planned renewal forces a fresh Clerk token before opening the replacement stream. Concurrent token refresh requests within the authenticated tab share an in-flight refresh. This adds a Clerk request roughly every 25 seconds during continuous foreground use; it avoids treating a cached token near expiry as a renewed authorization period.
- A replacement's transient failure leaves a healthy current stream serving its consumers and does not itself show an interrupted-updates notice. Loss of the usable subscription, or failure to obtain a required current view snapshot, marks the affected views as potentially stale. Confirmed authentication loss, access denial and group deletion retain the existing access boundaries and close the affected channels; a candidate's mere network failure is not evidence of access denial.
- Manual retry on a healthy notification channel retries only the requesting consumer's data read. When the channel is unavailable, retry also requests shared connection recovery while preserving the single replacement limit. One consumer's failed read does not tear down a healthy stream used by other consumers.
- If the current stream reaches its deadline before the replacement receives `ready`, the server closes the current stream on time and the affected views become potentially stale. The already-running replacement attempt continues; its deadline is ten seconds from starting token acquisition through receiving `ready`. Token acquisition must obey that local attempt deadline even if the SDK's underlying refresh cannot be cancelled. A late completion from an abandoned attempt cannot open or promote a stream. A shared refresh is released when settled, and a new attempt may not extend its own deadline merely by joining that refresh.
- Failed connection attempts use the existing bounded retry intervals of 1, 2, 4, 8 and then at most 15 seconds. An unavailable channel retains at most one in-flight attempt. A current stream reaching EOF while its replacement is pending does not spawn a second attempt. Handoff, background shutdown and releasing a consumer are intentional lifecycle actions, not connection failures.
- A replacement `ready` promotes that stream and closes the old stream immediately, independently of REST read completion. Each consumer marks its pre-handoff reads obsolete, cancels obsolete shared-cache requests where applicable and obtains a current snapshot. Its read coordinator persists across connection replacements, serializes that consumer's reads and rereads when notifications arrive during a read. Aborted or obsolete results cannot update React state or repopulate the view's shared cache with an older snapshot. A consumer clears its stale status only after its latest required snapshot succeeds.

Shared streams and readers belong to the authenticated session. Releasing one consumer leaves other consumers subscribed; releasing the final consumer closes that channel. Signing out disposes the session's channels and prevents old reads or callbacks from reaching another account. These responsibilities preserve the existing authorization and navigation boundaries rather than introduce new domain rules.

Initial subscribers and consumers joining an already-ready shared channel subscribe before obtaining their view's current snapshot. A late subscriber does not wait for the next renewal to load. Group consumers refresh their own snapshots at initial readiness, handoff and foreground recovery. The member stream retains ADR-0017's names-version behavior: its `ready` payload is already a names-version snapshot, and unchanged versions do not force unrelated reads. Versions and stream ownership remain isolated to the authenticated session.

Hiding the tab cancels stream attempts and obsolete reads as well as closing current and replacement streams; late callbacks remain ignored. Foreground recovery performs one shared reconnect per channel even if visibility and focus events arrive together. Visible focus on a healthy channel requests current reads without deliberately breaking its stream. Unsent form state follows the existing draft-preservation and conflict rules in issue #19.

The owner accepted the interview recommendations and requested replacing the matching issue with the complete plan. Issue #23 is the actionable specification for this decision and supersedes its earlier proposal to hide all background failure notices: healthy handoff stays quiet, while genuine synchronization or snapshot failure remains visibly stale. Implementation and deployment verification are separate from acceptance of this decision.

## Validation

Extend the existing HTTP API tests with isolated PostgreSQL and controlled identities, and the browser scenarios using the real application and API. Assert observable behavior rather than private subscriber maps. Cover:

- Same-group consumers share one current stream and at most one replacement. A newly mounted consumer reads current data immediately after subscription; unmounting one leaves the others receiving updates, while the final unmount cleans up the channel.
- Planned renewal respects the earlier of verified JWT expiry and the 30-second ceiling, announces renewal five seconds ahead and closes the old stream only after replacement readiness or its own deadline. Forced token refresh obtains renewed authorization; same-expiry tokens and authentication failures cannot cause a rapid connection loop or extend the old stream.
- Changes around renewal and notifications during delayed reads lead to current snapshots. Duplicate invalidations are harmless, and a held old response cannot overwrite newer page state or caches. These reads and reconnections create no financial writes.
- Slow authentication or missing `ready` respects the ten-second attempt deadline, including late token completion. Candidate failure does not interrupt a healthy old stream; old-stream expiry during an attempt does not create an extra attempt. Real connection or snapshot failures retain appropriate stale presentation until successful recovery.
- Hidden tabs have no active streams or connection retries. Foreground recovery, rapid visibility/focus events, navigation, sign-out and switching accounts do not leak streams, apply old callbacks or discard unsent input. Group deletion and denied access close the relevant channels and preserve the existing protected-data behavior.

Compare renewal latency and first-current-snapshot timing under healthy connectivity, including changes near renewal boundaries and multiple screens. Report p50, p99, maximum, stale-notice flashes, subscription gaps and whether each screen converges to the database's latest state. The acceptance target is to remove the deliberate 1000 ms renewal pause and unnecessary interruption flashes; healthy overlap should retain a subscription across handoff. Fault recovery has no universal latency guarantee.

Verify streaming and real Clerk authentication through nginx and the intended public route, including FRP, as required by issue #19. Controlled test identities do not prove Clerk expiry behavior, and local nginx measurements do not prove public-route delivery. Report unavailable deployment checks explicitly rather than presenting them as passed. The research in [realtime-renewal-cases.md](../research/realtime-renewal-cases.md) supplies background evidence, not additional approved behavior or completed benchmark results.
